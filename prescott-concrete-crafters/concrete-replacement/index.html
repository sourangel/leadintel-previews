What's included.

When a slab has sunk, heaved or broken into pieces, a patch only hides the problem for a season. In Prescott the usual causes are freeze and thaw, water running under the slab, and a base that was never compacted. Replacing the concrete without fixing why it failed means doing the job twice.

  • Breaking out and hauling off old driveways, patios and walks
  • Finding out why the old slab failed before pouring a new one
  • Rebuilding the base so it is firm and drains
  • Fixing slopes that send water toward the house
  • New forms, reinforcement and a new pour
  • Tying the new work into what stays

Signs it is time to replace.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Slabs that have sunk, tilted or broken into pieces
  • Cracks that keep opening or have a height difference
  • Surface flaking or crumbling after winter
  • Water that runs toward the house instead of away

Replacement questions.

How do I know if I need replacement or repair?

If the slab is sound underneath, repair can work. If the base has washed out or the slab has broken up, replacement is usually the lasting fix. Someone should look at it before you decide.

What does freeze and thaw do to old concrete?

Water gets into small cracks, freezes, expands and breaks the surface apart. Prescott sees that cycle many times each winter, which is why scaling and flaking are common here.

Will the new concrete match the old?

New concrete does not match weathered concrete exactly. Where new meets old, expect some difference in color for a while.
