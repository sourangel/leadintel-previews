What's included.

In Arizona a patio is used most in the shoulder seasons and at night, and it faces full sun the rest of the year. Details decide how it ages: a slope away from the house, joints that follow the layout, a light finish that stays easier on bare feet, and a sealer that matches the surface. The finishing touches count too, such as lighting, water features and landscaping around the slab.

  • Custom patio design and layout
  • Plain, colored and stamped concrete patios
  • Space for lighting, water features and landscaping around the patio
  • Pads for outdoor fireplaces, fire pits and seating areas
  • Steps and walkways that connect the patio to the house and yard
  • Slope for drainage away from the house

Patios questions.

How big should a patio be?

Big enough for the furniture plus room to walk around it. A good rule is to lay out the table, chairs and grill on the grass first and mark the edges.

Will a concrete patio be too hot in summer?

Any slab in full Arizona sun gets hot. Lighter colors and broom textures stay cooler than dark, sealed surfaces, and shade from a ramada or trees helps most.

Can the patio be stamped or colored?

Yes. Plain, colored and stamped concrete are all common for patios, and stamped work is covered on its own page.

Do I need approval before building a patio?

Many HOAs in Goodyear want to approve a patio before it is built, and attached covers or outdoor fireplaces can bring in permits. Ask before work starts.
