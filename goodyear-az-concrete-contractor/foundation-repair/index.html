What's included.

In the West Valley the usual causes are water and soil. Monsoon runoff that runs toward the house, irrigation that soaks the ground beside the foundation, and soil that swells and shrinks all move slabs and footings. Hairline cracks in a slab are common and often harmless. Cracks that are wide, step up or down, or let doors and windows bind need a closer look, sometimes by a structural engineer, before anything is patched.

  • Look at cracks, settling and drainage around the foundation

  • Crack repair and sealing
  • Fixing the water problem that caused the damage

  • Advice on when an engineer's evaluation is needed

Signs a foundation needs a look.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a quarter inch, or cracks that step up or down
  • Doors or windows that stick or no longer close
  • A slab or stem wall that has sunk, tilted or pulled apart
  • Water that runs toward the house or stands against it

Foundation repair questions.

Are all foundation cracks serious?

No. Hairline cracks from concrete shrinking as it cures are common. Wide cracks, cracks that keep growing, or cracks with a height difference deserve a closer look.

What causes foundation problems in Arizona?

Water is the main one: poor drainage, monsoon runoff and heavy irrigation next to the house. Soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry in parts of the Valley can add to it.

Do I need an engineer?

For wide or moving cracks, or if doors and windows are binding, an engineer's evaluation is the safest first step, and a repair plan can follow from it.

Can a foundation be fixed with a patch?

Only when the cause has been dealt with. Patching over a moving or wet foundation hides the problem for a season and then the crack comes back.
