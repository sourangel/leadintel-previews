Why this matters in the Phoenix-Scottsdale area

The hard part of an addition is where new meets old: the roofline, the slab, the electrical panel, the air conditioning and how the two parts of the house feel from room to room.

A Google reviewer who asked for an addition estimate said Raindance listened, considered the budget and brought ideas the family had not thought of.

What is included

  • Site visit and a conversation about what you want the space to do
  • Layout and design, with options within your budget
  • Permits and inspections
  • Foundation, framing, roof and exterior tie-in
  • Electrical, plumbing and air conditioning to serve the new space
  • Interior finishes and a final walkthrough

How it goes

  1. Visit

    We walk the house and the lot and listen to what you want the new space to do.

  2. Plan

    We settle the layout, the scope and the budget.

  3. Build

    Foundation through finishes, with the rest of your home kept as protected as the job allows.

  4. Walkthrough

    We go through the new space with you and finish the punch list.

Is an addition the right move?

  • You like your neighborhood and your lot but need more room
  • You have space on the lot and setbacks that allow it
  • You would rather add on than move
  • You want the addition to match the house

Questions about Additions

Do I need a permit for an addition?

Yes. Additions are permitted and inspected by your city or county. We handle the permit process as part of the job.

Will the addition match my house?

Matching roof lines, stucco and windows is part of planning an addition. We talk it through with you during design.

Can I stay in the house during construction?

Often yes, since much of the work happens outside the existing walls. We will tell you what to expect once we have seen the project.
