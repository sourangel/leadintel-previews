What's included.

Most driveway failures start under the concrete. In the Phoenix valley the ground is often hard caliche in one spot and soft fill in the next, so a base that was not prepared evenly settles, water finds the gap, and the slab cracks along the weak line. Thickness, joint spacing and finish timing all depend on what will drive on it and on the summer heat the day it is poured.

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Layout and grade

    Measure the drive, check where water needs to go, and mark the slope away from the garage and house.

  2. 2

    Base and forms

    Remove what has to go, compact the base and set forms to line and grade.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and float while the concrete is workable. In summer that means early starts and a plan for the heat.

  4. 4

    Joints, cure and seal

    Cut or tool joints where cracks would otherwise form, keep the surface from drying too fast, and seal when it is ready.

Signs it's time for a new driveway.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a pencil line, or cracks that have shifted
  • Sections that have sunk or tilted toward the garage
  • Standing water after a monsoon storm
  • Surface flaking, pitting or crumbling at the edges

Driveways questions.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars and light trucks. Heavier vehicles, RVs or soft soil call for more thickness, steel, or both. The right answer depends on what will actually park on it.

Can you pour a driveway in the Arizona summer?

Yes, with a plan. Concrete sets fast at 105 degrees, so pours start early, the mix and finishing are timed to the heat, and the surface is kept from drying out too quickly while it cures.

How long before I can drive on it?

Foot traffic is usually fine after a day or two. Cars and light trucks typically wait about seven days, and heavier vehicles longer. Ask for dates for your pour.

Does my HOA need to approve a new driveway?

Many communities in Surprise and Sun City do, especially for color, stamping or a changed footprint. Check your HOA rules before you pick a finish.
