What's included.

A patio looks simple, but the details decide how it ages: the slope that sends rain away from the house, joints placed on purpose, and a surface that is not slick when wet. Tucson's sun and monsoon rain work on a patio every year, so the finish and sealer choice matter. We talk through color and pattern before the pour.

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Plan the patio

    Size, slope, color and pattern are talked through before anything is poured.

  2. 2

    Prepare and form

    The area is excavated and compacted, and forms set the shape and the drainage.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Concrete is placed and finished, then colored, stained or stamped if you chose that.

  4. 4

    Cure and seal

    It cures slowly, then gets sealed to protect the color and the surface.

When a new patio makes sense.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • You want a patio, a covered seating area or space for a grill or fire pit
  • An old patio has cracked, sunk or stained
  • Rain runs toward the house
  • The surface is worn, slick or crumbling

Patios questions.

Can you do stamped and colored concrete?

Yes. Bee's Concrete LLC lists stamped concrete and colored and stained concrete for patios.

Can an existing patio be repaired or renovated?

Yes. Repair and renovation are both on the list. We tell you whether fixing it or replacing it makes more sense.

Is a concrete patio too hot in summer?

Concrete holds heat, but lighter colors and textured finishes stay more comfortable than dark, smooth ones. Ask about shade and color for your yard.
