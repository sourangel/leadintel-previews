What's included.

Panels lift when tree roots push up from below, when soil settles unevenly or when irrigation softens the base. A lip of even half an inch is enough to catch a toe. Grinding fixes the edge, not the cause, so a good fix starts with looking at why the panel moved. If a slab is cracked through or still sinking, grinding is the wrong tool and we say so.

Signs you have a trip hazard.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • A sidewalk panel that sits higher than its neighbor
  • A sharp lip where a driveway meets the sidewalk
  • Tree roots lifting a section of walkway
  • A slip-and-fall complaint, or a notice from your city or insurer

Trip hazard grinding questions.

Is grinding cheaper than replacing the panel?

Usually, by a wide margin. Grinding removes the lip in place, with no demolition, no new pour and no waiting for concrete to cure.

Will ground concrete look different?

The ground edge is lighter than the weathered surface around it at first and blends in over time. The goal is a safe, flush walking surface.

When is grinding the wrong fix?

If the slab has dropped a lot, is cracked through or keeps moving, grinding only hides the problem. In those cases we recommend repair or replacement and tell you why.
