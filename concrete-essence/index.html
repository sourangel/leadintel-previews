Questions we hear a lot.

How long until I can walk or drive on it?

Most slabs take foot traffic after about 24 to 48 hours. Cars and light trucks usually wait around seven days. Concrete keeps gaining strength for roughly 28 days, so ask for dates for your job.

Can concrete be poured in Arizona summer heat?

Yes, with a plan. When it is over 100 degrees, concrete sets fast and can crack if the surface dries too quickly. Crews pour early, adjust the mix, and keep the surface moist while it cures.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars. Heavier vehicles or weak soil call for more thickness, steel, or both. The slab should be sized to what will actually drive on it.

Do I need a permit in Goodyear?

It depends on the job. Foundations and structural work almost always need one, and work in the public right of way, like a sidewalk or driveway apron at the curb, is usually handled through the city. Check with the City of Goodyear and your HOA before the work starts.

How is concrete work priced?

By area, thickness, prep, access and finish. Ask for an itemized written quote so you can see what is driving the number.

Should concrete be sealed?

For most driveways and patios, yes. A sealer slows staining and surface wear, and the desert sun is hard on bare concrete. Ask which sealer suits your finish and when to reapply.
