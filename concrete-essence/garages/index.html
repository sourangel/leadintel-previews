What's included.

A garage slab needs a flat, firm base, enough thickness for the vehicles and loads it carries, and a slope that sends spilled water toward the door. It also meets the driveway at the apron and the house at the stem wall, and those joints need to be planned. A finish that is too smooth gets slippery when wet, and an unsealed floor soaks up oil.

  • Garage concrete for residential and commercial properties
  • Slabs for new garages, shops and additions
  • Base prep, forms and steel sized to the load
  • A slight slope toward the door
  • Joints planned around the stem wall and the driveway apron
  • A broom or trowel finish and curing, with sealing advice

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Check the plans and site

    Confirm slab thickness, slope, and how the floor meets the walls and the driveway.

  2. 2

    Prep and form

    Grade and compact the base, set forms and steel, and make sure everything is ready for inspection if one is required.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and finish the slab to a level, even surface with a finish that is safe when wet.

  4. 4

    Cut joints and cure

    Cut control joints on time, then cure the slab before vehicles and heavy tool chests go on it.

When to call.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • You are building a new garage, shop or addition
  • A garage floor has cracked, sunk or flakes at the surface
  • Water pools inside the garage
  • You want a floor ready for a sealer or coating

Garages questions.

How thick is a garage slab?

Four inches is common for a typical garage. Shops, heavier vehicles and weak soil may need more thickness or steel.

Should a garage floor be sealed?

Usually, yes. A sealer slows oil and stain absorption and makes cleanup easier. Ask which product suits your finish.

Do I need a permit for a garage slab?

A new garage or an addition almost always needs a permit, and the slab is inspected. Check with the City of Goodyear for your job.
