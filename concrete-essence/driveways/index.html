What's included.

Driveways fail from underneath. A soft or uneven base, a slab that is too thin for the vehicles on it, and joints cut too late or too far apart are what lead to cracks and settling. Arizona adds its own load: surface temperatures that climb well past 140 degrees in summer and monsoon downpours that test the drainage. The slab needs the right thickness, a good base, and a slope that sends water to the street.

  • Concrete driveways for homes and commercial properties
  • Layout, forms and grade set for drainage
  • Base prep and compaction
  • Thickness and steel sized to the vehicles that will use it
  • Control joints and a broom or decorative finish
  • Curing and a final walkthrough

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Plan the layout

    Set the width, slope and joint pattern, and check how the driveway meets the garage and the street.

  2. 2

    Prep and form

    Remove the old surface if needed, grade and compact the base, and set forms and steel.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place and screed the concrete, float it, and finish the surface. Pouring early in the day matters in summer.

  4. 4

    Joint, seal and cure

    Cut joints at the right time and cure the slab. Ask about a sealer to slow staining and sun wear.

When to call.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Your driveway has cracked, settled or heaved
  • Water runs toward the garage instead of the street
  • You are building a new home or adding a second drive
  • A commercial property needs a heavy-duty drive or apron

Driveways questions.

How thick should a concrete driveway be?

Four inches is common for passenger cars. Heavier vehicles, RVs or weak soil call for more thickness, steel, or both.

How long before I can drive on a new driveway?

Foot traffic is usually fine after a day or two. Cars and light trucks typically wait about seven days, and the concrete keeps gaining strength for roughly 28 days.

Does my HOA need to approve a driveway?

Many Goodyear neighborhoods have an HOA, and some require approval for new or changed driveways, colors and finishes. Check before you order the work.
