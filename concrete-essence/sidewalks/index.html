What's included.

Sidewalks fail at the joints and the edges. Panels that are too long crack in random places, a soft base settles under the first heavy load, and slabs without enough slope hold water at the door. A good sidewalk starts with a firm, graded base and ends with joints cut where you want the cracks to go. Work at the curb or in the public right of way usually goes through the city, so permits come first.

  • Sidewalks and walkways for homes and commercial properties
  • Layout and forms set to line, grade and slope
  • Base prep and compaction before the pour
  • Control joints cut so cracks follow the joints
  • A broom or other non-slip finish, then proper curing
  • Cleanup and a walkthrough when the job is done

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Measure and lay out

    Walk the route, set the width and slope, and mark where joints and any tie-ins to existing concrete will go.

  2. 2

    Prep the base

    Strip, grade and compact the base so the sidewalk sits on firm, even ground that drains away from the building.

  3. 3

    Form and pour

    Set forms to line and level, place the concrete, screed and float it, and finish the surface for grip.

  4. 4

    Joint and cure

    Cut or tool the joints, then keep the surface from drying too fast in the Arizona sun while it cures.

Sidewalks questions.

How wide should a sidewalk be?

A path to the front door is commonly about 36 to 48 inches wide. Public and commercial sidewalks have their own width and slope rules, so check with the city before you plan the job.

Why does a new sidewalk crack?

Concrete shrinks as it cures. Joints give it planned places to crack. Poor base prep, panels that are too long, or fast drying in desert heat can make it crack anywhere else.

Do I need a permit for a sidewalk in Goodyear?

Work in the public right of way generally needs city approval, while a private walkway on your own lot often does not. Check with the City of Goodyear and your HOA first.
