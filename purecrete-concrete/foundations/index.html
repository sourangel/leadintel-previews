What's included.

A foundation cannot be adjusted after it cures, so the work that matters happens before the truck arrives: soil and base prep, forms set to line and level, steel where the plan says, and a mix ordered for the load. Valley soils vary from hard caliche to expansive clay, so the engineer's plan and the city inspection are what protect the slab.

  • Footings, stem walls and slabs on grade
  • Slabs for garages, shops and outbuildings
  • Excavation and compacted base
  • Steel placed to the plan and ready for inspection
  • Placed, screeded, floated and finished
  • Curing before anything loads the slab

When a foundation is on the list.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • You are building a new home, garage, shop or addition
  • You need a pad for a detached structure or equipment
  • An existing slab has settled, tilted or cracked through
  • A previous pour failed an inspection

Foundations questions.

Do I need a permit for a foundation?

Almost always. Foundations are structural, so cities inspect them. The permit office in Peoria tells you what your job needs.

How long does a foundation take to cure?

A slab is usually walkable within a day or two and keeps gaining strength for about 28 days. Your builder or engineer decides when framing can load it.

Why does Arizona soil matter?

Caliche is hard and uneven to dig, and expansive clay moves with moisture. A good plan accounts for both with the right base and steel.
