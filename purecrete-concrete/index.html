How we keep it honest.

Concrete is hard to judge until it's too late to change. So we put the important parts in front of you early.

  1. Talk it through first.

    Call or text what you want built and where. Photos of the spot help.

  2. Settle the scope before the pour.

    Size, thickness, finish and access should be agreed before a truck is scheduled. Ask for them in writing.

  3. Check the license.

    Arizona contractors are licensed by the Registrar of Contractors. Look up any contractor at roc.az.gov before you sign.

  4. Ask about the mix.

    Water added at the truck weakens concrete. Ask what mix is ordered and who decides if it changes.

Questions we hear a lot.

How long until I can walk or drive on it?

Most slabs take foot traffic after about 24 to 48 hours. Cars and light trucks usually wait around seven days. Concrete keeps gaining strength for roughly 28 days.

Can concrete be poured in Arizona summer heat?

Yes, with a plan. Pours start early, the mix and timing are adjusted for heat, and the surface is kept from drying too fast so it does not crack.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars. Heavier vehicles or weak soil call for more thickness, steel, or both.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on the city and the job. Foundations, additions and structural work almost always do. Contact the permit office in Peoria for your address.

How do you price a job?

By area, thickness, prep, access and finish. Call or text for an estimate.

Should concrete be sealed?

For most driveways and patios, yes. A sealer slows staining and surface wear. Reseal as the product recommends.
