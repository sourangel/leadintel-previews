What's included.

Sidewalks fail at the edges and the joints: a base that settles, a slope that holds water, or tree roots nobody planned around. Public sidewalks and anything that ties into a street or curb are in the right of way, so they usually need a city permit and follow city standards for width, slope and ramps.

  • City sidewalks and walks, new and replacement
  • Maintenance and replacement of cracked or lifted sections
  • Front walks and garden paths
  • Grading so water drains away from the walk and the house
  • Joints planned for the layout
  • Broom or decorative finishes

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Mark the line

    Walk the route, mark the layout and check slope and drainage.

  2. 2

    Prep and form

    Excavate, compact the base and set forms to the line.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place and finish with a grip surface, then tool the joints where they belong.

  4. 4

    Cure and protect

    Keep it from drying too fast and keep traffic off until it is ready.

Signs a walk needs work.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Sections that have lifted into a trip edge
  • Standing water that never clears
  • Cracks that have moved or opened
  • A worn surface that is slippery when wet

City walks questions.

Do I need a permit for a sidewalk?

A walk on your own property usually does not. A sidewalk in the public right of way, or one that ties into the street or a curb, usually does. Peoria can confirm for your address.

Who is responsible for the sidewalk in front of my house?

It depends on the city and on where the sidewalk sits. Check with Peoria before you replace a public walk.

How soon can I walk on it?

Most walks take foot traffic after a day or two.
