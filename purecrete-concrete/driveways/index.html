What's included.

Most driveway failures start under the concrete. A base that was not compacted settles, water finds the gap, and the slab cracks along the weak line. In the valley, summer heat and sudden monsoon rain add stress, so slope, joints and finish timing matter as much as thickness.

  • New concrete driveways
  • Repair and replacement of failed driveways
  • Grading and slope so water runs away from the house and garage
  • Compacted base and reinforcement sized to what will park on it
  • Control joints planned before the pour
  • Broom, smooth, colored or decorative finishes

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Walk the site

    Measure, check drainage and access, and note anything under the slab that has to be fixed first.

  2. 2

    Prep the base

    Excavate, compact the base, set forms to line and grade, and place steel.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and float, finish for grip, then cut or tool the joints.

  4. 4

    Cure and seal

    Keep the surface from drying too fast in the heat, keep traffic off, and seal when it is ready.

Signs it is time for a new driveway.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a pencil line, or cracks that have shifted
  • Sections that have sunk or tilted toward the house
  • Standing water after a monsoon storm
  • Surface flaking, pitting or crumbling edges

Driveways questions.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars and light trucks. Heavier vehicles or soft soil call for more thickness, steel, or both.

How long before I can drive on a new driveway?

Foot traffic is usually fine after a day or two. Cars and light trucks typically wait about seven days, and heavy vehicles longer.

Do I need a permit for the driveway apron?

Work in the public right of way, such as the apron at the street, usually needs a permit from the city. Ask Peoria's permit office what applies to your address.
