Tree borders in Surprise, AZ

A tree border is a concrete ring set around a tree or a planting bed, cut into the lawn or the rock. Extreme Borders and Curbs builds tree borders as part of its landscape curbing work in Surprise and across the northwest Phoenix valley.

What's included.

A tree in the middle of a lawn means trimming around the trunk and mower hits on the bark. A border gives it a clean edge, keeps rock or mulch in the bed and holds turf back. Size matters most: the ring should leave room for the trunk and root flare to grow, so plan it for the tree it will be in ten years, not the one you planted.

  • Round, oval and free-form rings around trees and palms
  • Borders around planting beds and shrubs
  • The same styles, stamped patterns and colors as the rest of your curbing
  • Rings that tie into the border along the lawn, so the yard reads as one design
  • Residential and commercial properties

When a tree needs a border.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Trimming or mowing around trunks every week
  • Rock or mulch drifting out of the tree well
  • Turf growing right up to the trunk
  • A plastic or brick ring that has popped out of place

Tree borders questions.

How big should a tree border be?

Bigger than the tree is today. The ring should sit well clear of the trunk and leave room for the root flare. For a young tree, a wide ring now saves a tear-out later.

Will a concrete border hurt the tree?

A border set well outside the trunk should not. Concrete should never be placed tight against a trunk, and the ring needs room for the tree to grow.

Can it match my other borders?

Yes. Tree borders come in the same styles, patterns and colors as the rest of the curbing.

What goes inside the ring?

Most Surprise yards use decorative rock or mulch. Keep both off the trunk itself so the bark stays dry.
