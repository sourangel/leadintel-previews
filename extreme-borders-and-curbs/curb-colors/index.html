What's included.

Color is the decision people second-guess most. Concrete reads differently wet and dry, in morning light and afternoon sun, and next to your own rock. In Arizona the sun slowly softens any outdoor color, so pick a tone that looks right against your decorative gravel and the stucco on the house, and look at the sample outside, not under indoor light.

  • A color sample board with many colors, including Sangria, Sunset Rose and Spanish Gold
  • Any color with any stamped pattern and curbing profile
  • Colors chosen to sit beside desert rock, stucco and turf
  • Residential and commercial properties

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Look at the board

    Start with the color sample board and narrow it to two or three.

  2. 2

    Test against your rock

    Hold the samples next to your gravel and the house, outside, wet and dry.

  3. 3

    Pair with pattern and profile

    Make sure color, stamp and curbing style work together.

  4. 4

    Place and cure

    The color goes in with the border, which then cures before it takes traffic.

When color is the question.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • A gray border clashes with the rock or the house
  • Your HOA has an approved palette
  • You want a border that blends in, or one that stands out
  • A new rock bed needs a color to match

Color choices questions.

Which colors can I choose?

The color sample board includes Sangria, Sunset Rose, Spanish Gold and many more.

Will the color match my rock?

Hold a sample against your rock in your own light, wet and dry, before you decide.

Does color fade in the Arizona sun?

All outdoor color changes slowly with sun and wear. Ask which colors hold up best in valley sun and whether a sealer is recommended.
