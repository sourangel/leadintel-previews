What's included.

A foundation cannot be adjusted after it cures, so the work that matters happens before the truck arrives: soil and base prep, forms set to line and level, steel where the plan says, and a mix ordered for the load. We work from your plans or your engineer's drawings and schedule the inspections your city requires.

  • Footings, stem walls and monolithic slabs
  • Slabs on grade for garages, shops, additions and outbuildings
  • Excavation, compacted base and vapor barrier prep
  • Rebar and mesh placed on chairs and ready for inspection
  • Placed, screeded, floated and finished on schedule with enough crew
  • Curing, then a walkthrough with you

Foundations questions.

Do I need a permit for a foundation?

Almost always, yes. Foundations are structural, so cities inspect them. We tell you what your job needs and schedule the inspections we are responsible for.

How long does a foundation take to cure?

A slab is usually walkable within a day or two and keeps gaining strength for about 28 days. Your builder or engineer decides when framing can load it. We give you dates for your job.

Can you work from my engineer's plans?

Yes. We build to the drawings. If something on site does not match the plan, we flag it before we pour, not after.
