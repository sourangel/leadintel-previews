What's included.

A patio looks simple, but the details decide how it ages: slope away from the house, joints that follow the layout, a finish that is not slick when wet, and a sealer that matches the surface. We talk through color and pattern with samples before anything is poured.

  • Broom-finish, colored and stamped patios
  • Exposed aggregate and decorative borders
  • Pads for fire pits, outdoor kitchens and hot tubs, built with the right base and steel
  • Steps and walkways that connect to the patio
  • Sloped for drainage away from the house
  • Sealing for color and stain protection

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Plan and choose

    We lay out the size, slope, color and pattern, and show you samples.

  2. 2

    Prep and form

    Excavate, compact the base, set forms and place steel for the load.

  3. 3

    Pour, color and stamp

    Place and finish, apply color, and stamp or texture while the surface is ready.

  4. 4

    Cure and seal

    Cure it slowly, then seal to protect the color and the surface.

When a new patio makes sense.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • You want a patio, fire pit pad or outdoor kitchen
  • An old patio has cracked, sunk or stained
  • Water runs toward the house
  • The surface is slippery or crumbling

Patios questions.

How long does stamped concrete last?

A properly built stamped patio can last for many years. Resealing every few years keeps the color and pattern looking fresh.

Can you add color to concrete?

Yes. Color can be mixed in, applied to the surface, or both. We show you samples in your own light before you choose.

Is stamped concrete slippery?

It can be when wet, so we choose textures and a sealer with grip for areas around pools and steps.
