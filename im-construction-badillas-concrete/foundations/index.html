What's included.

A foundation cannot be adjusted after it cures, so the work that matters happens before the truck arrives. In the Phoenix valley that means checking the soil report, because some West Valley soils swell and shrink with moisture and a slab designed for them is not the same as one on stable ground. Cities inspect foundations before concrete goes in, and the plans come from your engineer or builder.

  • Footings, stem walls and slabs on grade
  • Slabs for garages, additions and outbuildings
  • Concrete work built to your plans and your engineer's soil recommendations
  • Post-tension slabs where the plans call for them (see the post-tension page)
  • Concrete placed, screeded and finished, then cured

Foundations questions.

Do I need a permit for a foundation?

Almost always, yes. Foundations are structural, so cities inspect them. Ask what your job needs and who schedules each inspection.

How long does a foundation take to cure?

A slab is usually walkable within a day or two and keeps gaining strength for about 28 days. Your builder or engineer decides when framing can load it.

Why do West Valley foundations often use post-tension steel?

Some valley soils are expansive, and tensioned cables help a slab ride out seasonal movement. Your engineer decides whether your lot needs it.
