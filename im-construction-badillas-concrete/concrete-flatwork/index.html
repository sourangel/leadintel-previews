What's included.

Flat concrete is simple to describe and unforgiving to get wrong. It has to drain, so slope matters. It has to crack in a straight line where you choose, so joints matter. And in the valley it has to survive surface temperatures that can top 150 degrees in summer, so curing matters. Most flatwork complaints trace back to base prep, joint layout or finishing in the heat.

  • Walkways, pads and patio slabs
  • Garage floors and shop floors
  • Broom, smooth and other standard finishes
  • Slope for drainage away from the house
  • Control joints laid out before the pour
  • Curing so the surface does not dry too fast

Signs flatwork needs attention.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a pencil line, or cracks that have shifted
  • Sections that have sunk, tilted or lifted into a trip edge
  • Standing water after rain
  • A surface that flakes, pits or crumbles

Flatwork questions.

What is the difference between flatwork and a foundation?

Flatwork is slab concrete that is not holding up a structure: walks, patios, driveways, floors. Foundations are structural and are inspected by the city.

Why does new concrete crack?

All concrete shrinks as it cures. Joints are cut or tooled so the cracking happens along a straight line you chose instead of wherever it wants.

Can I pour in July?

Yes, with a plan: early pours, a mix suited to heat, and curing that keeps the surface from drying too fast.
