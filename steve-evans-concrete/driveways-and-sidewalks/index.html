What's included.

Most driveway and sidewalk failures start below the slab. A base that was not compacted settles, water finds the gap, and the slab cracks along the weak line. In Yuma the sun adds to it: a slab placed in full summer sun can lose surface water faster than it can be finished, so timing and curing count. Sidewalks and driveway aprons in the public right of way usually need an encroachment permit from the city, so ask before the forms go in.

  • New concrete driveways and sidewalks
  • Tear-out and replacement of old, failed slabs
  • Compacted base and forms set to the grade you want
  • Joints planned before the pour
  • Broom or smooth finishes, with sealing as an option
  • Aprons and tie-ins to the street or sidewalk where the city allows

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Walk the site

    Measure, check drainage and access, and note anything under the old slab that has to be fixed first.

  2. 2

    Prep the base

    Break out the old concrete if there is any, compact the base and set forms to line and grade.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and float. Finish for grip, then tool or cut the joints.

  4. 4

    Cure and protect

    Keep the surface from drying too fast and keep traffic off until it has strength.

Signs it is time for a new slab.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a pencil line, or cracks that have shifted
  • Sections that have sunk, lifted or tilted
  • Standing water after rain or irrigation
  • Flaking, pitting or crumbling at the surface and edges

Driveways questions.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars and light trucks. Heavier vehicles or weak soil call for more thickness, steel, or both. Ask what will drive on it before you get a number.

How long before I can drive on it?

Foot traffic is usually fine after a day or two. Cars and light trucks typically wait about seven days, and heavy vehicles longer.

Can an old driveway be replaced instead of patched?

Yes. The old slab is broken out and hauled off, the base is fixed, and new concrete goes in. If the slab is sound underneath, a repair can make more sense.

Do I need a permit for a sidewalk?

A walk on your own lot usually does not. Anything in the public right of way, or tying into the street, curb or a city sidewalk, usually needs an encroachment permit.
