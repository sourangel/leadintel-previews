What's included.

In the Yuma area the ground is mostly river-deposited sand, silt and clay, and a lot of it sits on or next to irrigated farmland. Ground that stays damp can settle unevenly, and salty or sulfate-bearing soil can attack concrete over the years, so the base, the mix and the drainage around the slab get decided before the truck arrives. Heat is the other limit. In a Yuma summer concrete stiffens fast, so large placements are planned for the cool part of the day.

  • Poured concrete foundations and slabs on grade
  • Footings and stem walls where the plans call for them
  • Excavation, compacted base and forms set to line and level
  • Placing, screeding, floating and finishing the slab
  • Curing before anything loads it

Foundations questions.

Do I need a permit for a foundation?

Almost always. Foundations are structural, so the city or county inspects them. Ask your contractor which inspections your job needs and who schedules them.

How long does a slab take to cure?

A slab is usually walkable within a day or two and keeps gaining strength for about 28 days. Your builder or engineer decides when framing can load it.

Does Yuma heat change how a slab is poured?

Yes. Hot, dry, windy days pull water out of the surface fast, which can cause cracking. Pours are planned for early hours, and the surface is kept from drying too quickly while it cures.
