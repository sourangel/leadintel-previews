Questions we hear a lot.

How long until I can walk or drive on it?

Most slabs take foot traffic after about 24 to 48 hours. Cars and light trucks usually wait around seven days. Concrete keeps gaining strength for roughly 28 days, so ask for dates for your job.

How does Yuma heat affect a concrete pour?

Hot, dry, windy weather pulls water out of the surface fast, which can cause cracking. Summer pours are planned for early hours, and the surface is kept from drying too quickly while it cures.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars. Heavier vehicles or weak soil call for more thickness, steel, or both. Ask what will actually drive on it.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on the job. Foundations and structural work almost always do, and work in the public right of way, like a sidewalk or driveway apron, usually needs an encroachment permit from the city.

How do I get a price?

Call and describe the job. Price depends on area, thickness, prep, access and finish, so ask for a written quote and what is driving the number.

Should concrete be sealed?

For many patios and decorative surfaces, yes. A sealer slows staining and surface wear. Ask what suits your finish and when to reapply.
