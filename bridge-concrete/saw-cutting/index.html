What's included.

Concrete shrinks as it cures and cracks where it is weakest. Control joints tell it where to crack: cut in a straight line, at the right depth and at the right time, the crack follows the joint instead of wandering across the slab. When old concrete has to come out or a new slab has to tie in, a clean saw line gives a square edge to pour against.

  • Saw cutting concrete
  • Control joints in new slabs
  • Straight cuts for tie-ins to existing concrete
  • Cutting out sections before replacement
  • Cuts that pair with our flatwork and site concrete

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Mark the line

    Lay out the cut line to your plan or to where the slab needs to relieve stress.

  2. 2

    Set the depth

    Set the blade depth for the slab thickness and the purpose of the cut.

  3. 3

    Cut it clean

    Cut the line straight at the depth the slab needs.

  4. 4

    Pour or finish

    Tie in new concrete against the cut edge, or leave the joint as the relief line.

Saw cutting questions.

Why cut joints into new concrete?

Concrete shrinks as it dries. A control joint gives the crack a straight place to form so the slab does not crack at random.

Can you cut out part of a slab?

Saw cutting is how you get a clean edge for a partial removal. Ask us about your slab.

Do you pour the replacement too?

Yes. Saw cutting sits alongside our flatwork and foundation pours.
