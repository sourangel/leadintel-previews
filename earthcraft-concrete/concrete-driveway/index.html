What's included.

A driveway in the Valley takes summer heat above 110 degrees, monsoon runoff and the weight of trucks and trash bins. What decides how it ages is the work under and around it: a compacted base, forms set to drain water away from the garage, joints placed where the slab will crack on its own terms, and a finish that suits the sun. Pick the look you want, then make sure the base and joints get the same attention.

When a driveway needs replacing.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wide enough to catch a coin, or sections that have shifted
  • Water ponds against the garage door instead of running away
  • The surface is flaking, pitted or stained beyond cleaning
  • You are adding a garage, RV space or a wider approach

Driveways questions.

Which driveway finish holds up best in Arizona heat?

Plain broom-finished concrete is the simplest and holds up well. Stamped and colored surfaces look great too when they are sealed and the sealer is renewed. Ask which finish suits your home and sun exposure.

What does a concrete and artificial turf driveway look like?

Strips or squares of concrete with synthetic turf between them. It gives you green without watering, and it uses less solid concrete than a full slab.

How long before I can park on a new driveway?

Most slabs take foot traffic in a day or two. Cars usually wait about a week. Concrete keeps gaining strength for around 28 days, so ask for dates on your job.

Does a driveway need a permit in Tempe?

Work that touches the street apron or sidewalk is usually in the city right of way and can need a permit. Rules differ by city, so check with your city or ask us when you call.
