What's included.

A slab cannot be adjusted after it cures, so the work that counts happens before the truck arrives. In the Tucson area that means reading the soil (caliche, sand or expansive clay), compacting the base, setting forms to line and grade, placing steel where the plan calls for it, and planning the pour around summer heat.

  • Slabs on grade for homes, garages, shops and commercial buildings
  • Footings and stem walls
  • Foundations for new construction and additions
  • Forms set to line and grade, with steel placed ahead of inspection
  • Placed, screeded and finished by a crew sized to the pour
  • Work on occupied and operating sites, and multi-phase jobs

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Review the plans

    We read the drawings, check access and soil, and confirm which inspections your jurisdiction requires.

  2. 2

    Prep and form

    Compact the base, set forms to line and grade, and place steel on chairs.

  3. 3

    Inspect

    The inspection happens before the pour, so nothing gets buried that should not be.

  4. 4

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and finish while the concrete is workable, then cure it before it takes load.

Slabs questions.

Do I need a permit for a foundation?

Almost always. Foundations are structural, so Pima County and the City of Tucson inspect them. Ask for the inspections your job needs when you get your estimate.

Can the pour happen in summer heat?

Yes, with a plan. Tucson summers run well over 100 degrees, so large pours usually start early, and the surface has to be kept from drying too fast.

Can you work from my engineer's plans?

Foundations are built to the drawings. If something on site does not match the plans, that gets sorted out before concrete is placed.
