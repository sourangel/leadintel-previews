What's included.

Most driveway failures start under the concrete. A base that was not compacted settles, water finds the gap, and the slab cracks along the weak line. Tucson soil varies a lot from lot to lot, and monsoon rain tests the drainage every summer.

  • New concrete driveways and replacement of failed ones
  • Grading and slope so water runs away from the house and garage
  • Compacted base and reinforcement sized to what parks on it
  • Control joints planned before the pour
  • Broom, smooth or decorative finishes
  • Aprons and tie-ins where your jurisdiction allows

How we do it.

  1. 1

    Look at the site

    We measure, check drainage and access, and note anything that has to be fixed first.

  2. 2

    Prep the base

    Excavate, compact the base, set forms to line and grade, and place steel.

  3. 3

    Pour and finish

    Place, screed and float on schedule, finish for grip, then cut or tool the joints.

  4. 4

    Cure and seal

    Keep the surface from drying too fast and protect it from traffic.

Signs it's time for a new driveway.

If any of these sound familiar, the sooner someone looks at it the cheaper it usually is to fix.

  • Cracks wider than a pencil line, or cracks that have shifted
  • Sections that have sunk or tilted toward the house
  • Standing water after rain
  • Surface flaking or crumbling at the edges

Driveways questions.

How thick should a driveway be?

Four inches is common for cars and light trucks. Heavier vehicles or soft soil call for more thickness, steel, or both.

How long before I can drive on it?

Foot traffic is usually fine after a day or two. Cars and light trucks typically wait about a week. Ask for the date on your job.

Can you replace my old driveway instead of patching it?

Often, yes. The old slab is broken out, the base fixed and new concrete poured. A visit tells you whether a repair would hold.
